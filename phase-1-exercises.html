Phase 1 · Weeks 1–4

Foundation Exercises

Learn the exact movements used in Phase 1. Focus on clean technique and control. Load is secondary — movement quality comes first.

← Back to program overview

Form first: Use lighter weights until the movement feels automatic. If anything causes sharp pain (especially low back or knees), stop and regress the exercise.
Legs · Squat pattern

Goblet Squat

Primary: Quads, glutes · Secondary: Core, upper back

Setup

  1. Hold a dumbbell vertically against your chest, elbows tucked under the weight.
  2. Feet roughly shoulder-width, toes turned slightly out.
  3. Brace your core and keep your chest tall.

Execution

  1. Sit the hips back and down while keeping the elbows between the knees at the bottom.
  2. Descend until thighs are at least parallel (or as deep as mobility allows with a neutral spine).
  3. Drive through the mid-foot to stand, squeezing the glutes at the top.
Key cues
  • “Chest up, elbows down”
  • “Knees track over toes”
  • “Weight stays on mid-foot”
Common mistakes
  • Letting the torso collapse forward
  • Heels lifting off the floor
  • Knees caving inward
Posterior chain · Hinge

Romanian Deadlift (RDL)

Primary: Hamstrings, glutes · Secondary: Erectors, upper back

Setup

  1. Hold dumbbells (or a barbell) in front of the thighs with a neutral grip.
  2. Feet hip-width, soft bend in the knees.
  3. Pull the shoulders down and back; brace the core.

Execution

  1. Hinge at the hips by pushing the hips backward while the torso leans forward.
  2. Keep the bar/dumbbells close to the legs the entire time.
  3. Lower until you feel a strong stretch in the hamstrings (usually mid-shin), then drive the hips forward to stand.
Key cues
  • “Push the hips back like closing a car door”
  • “Keep the bar glued to the legs”
  • “Spine stays long and neutral”
Common mistakes
  • Rounding the lower back
  • Turning it into a squat (too much knee bend)
  • Letting the weights drift away from the body
Legs · Unilateral

Walking Lunges

Primary: Quads, glutes · Secondary: Core, hip stabilizers

Setup

  1. Stand tall holding dumbbells at your sides (or bodyweight).
  2. Core braced, eyes forward.

Execution

  1. Step forward with one leg and lower until both knees are roughly 90 degrees.
  2. Front knee tracks over the mid-foot; back knee drops toward the floor without slamming.
  3. Drive through the front heel to step the back leg forward into the next lunge.
Key cues
  • “Long, controlled steps”
  • “Torso stays upright”
  • “Front knee doesn’t cave inward”
Common mistakes
  • Taking tiny steps (turns into a split squat)
  • Leaning forward excessively
  • Letting the front knee drift past the toes aggressively
Shoulders · Overhead

Dumbbell Push Press

Primary: Shoulders, triceps · Secondary: Legs (dip drive), core

Setup

  1. Hold dumbbells at shoulder height, elbows slightly in front of the body.
  2. Feet shoulder-width, core tight.

Execution

  1. Dip the knees a few inches (quarter squat), then explosively drive the legs while pressing the dumbbells overhead.
  2. Finish with arms locked out, biceps near the ears, and a solid core brace.
  3. Lower the weights under control back to the shoulders.
Key cues
  • “Dip and drive — legs help the press”
  • “Finish tall with locked elbows”
  • “Ribs stay down”
Common mistakes
  • Excessive back arch at the top
  • Pressing the weights forward instead of straight up
  • Not using the leg drive (turns into a strict press)
Back · Horizontal pull

Dumbbell Row & Bent-Over Row

Primary: Lats, rhomboids, rear delts · Secondary: Biceps, core

One-Arm DB Row

  1. Place one hand and knee on a bench, other foot on the floor. Hold a dumbbell in the free hand.
  2. Keep the spine neutral and pull the dumbbell toward the hip, leading with the elbow.
  3. Squeeze the shoulder blade at the top, then lower under control.

Two-Arm Bent-Over Row

  1. Hinge forward ~45 degrees with a soft knee bend, holding dumbbells or a barbell.
  2. Pull the weights toward the lower ribs / upper abdomen while keeping the torso still.
  3. Control the eccentric; avoid using momentum.
Key cues
  • “Elbow to hip, not out to the side”
  • “Chest stays open”
  • “Squeeze the shoulder blade at the top”
Common mistakes
  • Rounding the upper back
  • Shrugging the shoulders toward the ears
  • Using too much body English
Core & Posterior

Plank · Glute Bridge · Bird Dog · Dead Bug · Superman

Plank

Forearms on the floor, body in a straight line from head to heels. Brace the core hard, squeeze the glutes, and avoid letting the hips sag or pike. Breathe steadily.

Glute Bridge

Lie on your back, knees bent, feet flat. Drive through the heels to lift the hips until the body forms a straight line from shoulders to knees. Squeeze the glutes hard at the top, then lower under control.

Bird Dog

On all fours, extend one arm forward and the opposite leg backward while keeping the hips level and the core braced. Pause briefly, then return and switch sides. Move slowly.

Dead Bug

Lie on your back, arms up, knees bent 90°. Slowly extend one arm and the opposite leg while pressing the low back into the floor. Return and switch. The key is keeping the lower back flat the entire time.

Superman

Lie face-down, arms extended. Simultaneously lift the arms, chest, and legs a few inches off the floor while squeezing the glutes and upper back. Hold briefly, then lower with control.

Back · Vertical pull

Lat Pulldown

Primary: Lats · Secondary: Biceps, rear delts, upper back

Setup & Execution

  1. Sit tall, thighs secured under the pad. Grip the bar slightly wider than shoulders.
  2. Initiate the pull by driving the elbows down and back, bringing the bar to the upper chest.
  3. Squeeze the lats at the bottom, then control the return to a full stretch at the top.
Key cues
  • “Pull the elbows to the hips”
  • “Chest up to meet the bar”
  • “Full stretch at the top every rep”
Common mistakes
  • Leaning too far back and turning it into a row
  • Using momentum / bouncing
  • Pulling the bar behind the neck
