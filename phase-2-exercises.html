Phase 2 · Weeks 5–8

Build Phase Exercises

Barbell compounds arrive. Heavier loads, tighter technique standards. Master these patterns before the Peak phase.

← Back to program overview

Progression note: Phase 2 introduces the barbell. Start conservatively. Film yourself from the side on the first few sessions of Back Squat and RDL.
Legs · Primary compound

Back Squat

Primary: Quads, glutes · Secondary: Core, upper back, adductors

Setup

  1. Bar sits on the upper traps (high-bar) or rear delts (low-bar). Unrack with the chest up and core braced.
  2. Step back, feet roughly shoulder-width, toes slightly out.
  3. Take a big breath into the belly and brace hard before descending.

Execution

  1. Break at the hips and knees simultaneously. Keep the torso as upright as your mobility allows.
  2. Descend until the hip crease is at or below the top of the knee (or as deep as you can with a neutral spine).
  3. Drive the floor away, keep the knees tracking over the toes, and finish tall with the glutes.
Key cues
  • “Brace before you move”
  • “Knees out over the toes”
  • “Drive the floor down”
Common mistakes
  • Butt wink (pelvis tucking under at the bottom)
  • Knees collapsing inward
  • Rising hips faster than the chest (good-morning pattern)
Legs · Quad emphasis

Front Squat

Primary: Quads · Secondary: Core, upper back, glutes

Setup

  1. Bar rests on the front delts. Elbows high, upper arms parallel to the floor (clean grip or cross-arm grip).
  2. Unrack, step back, feet shoulder-width.
  3. Brace the core and keep the elbows pointed forward throughout.

Execution

  1. Sit straight down between the hips while keeping the elbows high.
  2. Depth target is the same as the back squat — hip crease at or below the knee if mobility allows.
  3. Drive up while actively pushing the elbows toward the ceiling.
Key cues
  • “Elbows high the entire time”
  • “Chest stays tall”
  • “Sit between the hips”
Common mistakes
  • Elbows dropping (bar rolls forward)
  • Rounding the upper back
  • Shifting the weight onto the toes
Legs · Unilateral

Bulgarian Split Squat

Primary: Quads, glutes · Secondary: Hip stabilizers, core

Setup

  1. Rear foot elevated on a bench or box roughly knee height. Front foot far enough forward that the knee tracks over the mid-foot at the bottom.
  2. Hold dumbbells at the sides or a single dumbbell in goblet position.
  3. Torso stays mostly upright; a slight forward lean is fine.

Execution

  1. Lower under control until the front thigh is roughly parallel (or the back knee is close to the floor).
  2. Drive through the front mid-foot and heel to stand.
  3. Keep the front knee tracking in line with the toes.
Key cues
  • “Most of the load stays on the front leg”
  • “Front knee tracks over the mid-foot”
  • “Control the descent”
Common mistakes
  • Front foot too close (knee shoots past the toes aggressively)
  • Pushing hard off the back foot
  • Collapsing the torso forward
Back · Horizontal pull

Pendlay Row

Primary: Lats, upper back · Secondary: Erectors, biceps, rear delts

Setup & Execution

  1. Barbell starts on the floor. Hinge forward so the torso is roughly parallel to the ground, soft knee bend.
  2. Grip the bar just outside the legs. Brace the core hard.
  3. Explosively pull the bar to the lower chest / upper abdomen, then lower it all the way back to the floor under control (or let it settle fully between reps).
Key cues
  • “Torso stays parallel — no rising up”
  • “Pull explosively, lower with control”
  • “Bar starts and ends on the floor every rep”
Common mistakes
  • Using the hips and lower back to yank the weight
  • Standing up as the bar rises
  • Rounding the upper back heavily
Glutes · Isolation + strength

Hip Thrust

Primary: Glutes · Secondary: Hamstrings, core

Setup & Execution

  1. Upper back against a bench, feet flat on the floor roughly shoulder-width. Barbell or dumbbell across the hips (pad recommended).
  2. Drive through the heels to lift the hips until the torso is parallel to the floor and the glutes are fully contracted.
  3. Pause briefly at the top, then lower under control without letting the hips crash.
Key cues
  • “Chin tucked, eyes forward”
  • “Full glute squeeze at the top”
  • “Ribs down — no overarching”
Common mistakes
  • Hyperextending the lumbar spine at the top
  • Pushing through the toes instead of the heels
  • Letting the knees cave in
Back · Vertical pull

Pull-up (Band-Assisted or Weighted)

Primary: Lats · Secondary: Biceps, upper back, core

Setup & Execution

  1. Hang from a bar with a shoulder-width (or slightly wider) overhand grip. Start from a full dead hang.
  2. Initiate by depressing the shoulder blades, then pull the elbows down and back until the chin clears the bar.
  3. Lower under control to a full stretch. For band assist, loop the band over the bar and place a knee or foot in it. For weighted, use a dip belt or hold a dumbbell between the feet.
Key cues
  • “Pull the elbows to the hips”
  • “Full hang at the bottom every rep”
  • “No kipping or swinging”
Common mistakes
  • Only partial range of motion
  • Excessive swinging / kipping
  • Shrugging the shoulders at the top
Core

Russian Twist · Hanging Knee Raise · Side Plank · Cable Wood Chop

Russian Twist

Sit with knees bent, lean the torso back slightly, and rotate the shoulders side to side while holding a light plate or dumbbell. Keep the movement controlled — the goal is rotational control, not speed.

Hanging Knee Raise

Hang from a pull-up bar. Brace the core and lift the knees toward the chest without swinging. Lower under control. Progress later to straight-leg raises or toes-to-bar.

Side Plank

Support on one forearm and the side of the bottom foot. Stack the hips and keep the body in a straight line. Hold without letting the hips drop.

Cable Wood Chop

Set a cable at high or mid height. Rotate the torso while pulling the handle across the body in a diagonal pattern. Keep the arms relatively straight and initiate from the core, not the arms.
