Solve with Genesis is the independent practice of Jonathan Alexander, built on the same work he did inside a generation and trading utility.

He was asked to build a security program with almost no guidance beyond a minimum bar: keep the generation control center compliant with NERC CIP. Everything past that had to be figured out from the business, not the technology.

That meant sitting with every unit — generation, trading, site development, legal, finance, HR, communications — to understand what each one did, who depended on them, and how much risk they could tolerate. From that map, one framework: NIST CSF. It had to meet every current compliance requirement and scale to whatever came next.

He built an audit function to report on both NERC and NIST, and turned a spreadsheet of more than a thousand disconnected findings into a living portfolio using NIST RMF and FAIR-style quantitative scoring. Spending followed loss exposure, not gut feel. He put in governance, including a solutions review board, in a culture that had never had one — and where a parent company regularly pushed products that did not fit. Most were rejected. Leadership backed the no because the why was clear.

The recurring fight, with auditors, vendors, and other departments, was the same: somebody wanted to spend money or enforce a rule without first understanding what the business needed. The work is translation. Executives think in outcomes. Engineers think in solutions. Decisions belong to the people accountable for them.

That is now the firm’s offer: CISO-level judgment, fractional, without a bigger security program than the risk calls for.
