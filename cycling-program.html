12-Week Progressive Program

Stationary Cycling & Strength Training

Three phases, twelve weeks, zero guesswork. A structured plan that balances progressive strength training with zone-based cycling workouts — designed to take you from base fitness to peak performance.

Safety Note: This is a general fitness program, not individualized medical advice. Consult a physician or qualified coach before starting if you are injured, pregnant, managing a medical condition, or new to vigorous exercise. Stop any exercise that causes sharp pain and seek professional guidance.

Getting Started

Equipment You'll Need

Everything required to complete all 12 weeks. Most items are found in a standard gym. If you train at home, see the substitutions section below.

Stationary Bike

Any indoor trainer, spin bike, or smart bike with adjustable resistance. HR monitor or power meter recommended for zone targeting.

Barbell & Plates

Olympic or standard barbell with enough plates to progressively load squats, rows, and presses through all three phases.

Dumbbells

A range of light to moderate dumbbells for goblet squats, rows, lunges, push presses, and accessory work.

Squat Rack

Power rack or squat stands with safety bars for back squats, front squats, and overhead pressing.

Pull-up Bar

For weighted and assisted pull-ups. A lat pulldown machine works as a substitute if pull-ups aren't available.

Bench & Platform

A flat bench for hip thrusts and supported rows, plus floor space for split squats and core work.

Resistance Bands

Pull-up assist bands and lighter bands for warm-up mobility, activation drills, and exercise substitutions.

Foam Roller & Mat

Foam roller for recovery and a yoga mat for floor-based core work, stretching, and mobility sessions.

Optional but recommended: A heart rate monitor (chest strap or watch) for accurate zone targeting, a cadence sensor for cycling drills, and a training log or app to track your sessions. If you know your FTP, use power-based zones; otherwise, RPE and heart rate work perfectly.
Program Structure

Three Phases of Progression

Each phase builds on the last. Weeks 4, 8, and 12 are deload or taper weeks — reduce volume, let your body adapt, and come back stronger.

Phase 1 — Foundation

Weeks 1–4 · Low intensity · Habit-building

Build your aerobic base and establish movement quality. Lighter loads, higher reps, and Zone 2 cycling to create a sustainable training habit.

  • Zone 2 endurance cycling with cadence and single-leg drills
  • Goblet squats, RDLs, dumbbell-based strength
  • 3–4 sets, moderate loads, focus on technique
  • Week 4 is a deload — reduce volume ~20%
View exercises & form cues →

Phase 2 — Build

Weeks 5–8 · Increased intensity · Heavier loads

Transition to barbell work and introduce structured intensity on the bike. Tempo and sweet spot intervals build sustainable power.

  • Back squats, front squats, Pendlay rows — heavier loads
  • Tempo and sweet spot cycling intervals
  • 4 sets, 5–8 rep range, 2–3 min rest
  • Week 8 is a deload — reduce volume and intensity
View exercises & form cues →

Phase 3 — Peak

Weeks 9–12 · Maximal training · VO2 & threshold

Maximize strength and aerobic power. Heavy low-rep strength work pairs with VO2 max and threshold cycling. Week 12 tapers to a peak test.

  • 5×3 heavy strength — near-maximal loads
  • VO2 max intervals (3–4 min @ Zone 5) and threshold sessions
  • Week 11 is peak volume; Week 12 tapers and tests
  • 20-minute FTP or max sustained effort test in Week 12

Intensity Guide

Cycling Power & Heart Rate Zones

Use power (% of FTP), heart rate (% of max HR), or RPE to gauge intensity. If you don't know your FTP or max HR, RPE is your primary guide.

Zone% FTP% Max HRRPEFeel & Purpose
Zone 1 — Recovery< 55%< 68%1–2Very easy, conversation pace, active recovery
Zone 2 — Endurance56–75%69–79%4–5Easy aerobic, can hold a conversation, builds base
Zone 3 — Tempo76–90%80–89%6–7"Comfortably hard," sustainable for 30–60 min
Zone 4 — Sweet Spot / Threshold88–94%90–94%7–8Hard but sustained; just below threshold
Zone 5 — VO2 Max95–110%95–100%9–10Very hard, 3–8 min intervals, builds max aerobic power
RPE Scale: 1–3 Easy (recovery/warmup) · 4–6 Moderate (Zone 2 and tempo) · 7–10 Hard (sweet spot, threshold, VO2 max). Choose weights where the last 2 reps of each set are challenging but form stays clean.

Adaptability

Exercise Substitutions

Missing equipment or need a variation? Match the movement pattern (squat, hinge, push, pull) rather than copying the exact exercise.

Progressive Overload: Increase one variable at a time — load, reps, sets, or intensity zone. Small jumps beat big ones. Weeks 4, 8, and 12 reduce volume ~20–40% to let your body adapt and recover.

Track honestly. Progress patiently. Celebrate showing up.

The athletes who improve are the ones who stay consistent across all 12 weeks — not the ones who go hardest in week 1.
