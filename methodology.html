Methodology

Four steps. Same sequence, every engagement.

I start with the business, not the technology. The framework sets the bar; the business sets the pace.

01

Understand the business

What it depends on, what it can tolerate losing, and who's accountable for that decision. I sit with every unit that matters — operations, finance, legal, the board — and map the real dependencies and risk appetite before any control is proposed.

02

Map to one standard

A single framework that clears today's compliance bar and scales to tomorrow's. One standard means one language for the board, the auditors, and the engineers. NIST CSF is usually the right starting point for most organizations I work with.

03

Right-size the build

The framework sets the bar; the business sets the pace, unit by unit. Not every control needs to be mature on day one. We prioritize by actual loss exposure and implement in the order the business can absorb.

04

Govern it to last

Ownership, audit, and a review board — so decisions outlive any one champion. Governance is what keeps a program from becoming a collection of one-off projects that fade when the CISO leaves.

The through-line

Business drives security. Not the other way around.

Every decision is framed in terms the people accountable for the risk can understand and own. That is the only way a security program stays aligned with the actual risk the organization is willing to take.
