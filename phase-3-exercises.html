Phase 3 · Weeks 9–12

Peak Phase Exercises

Heavier loads, lower reps, and more demanding core work. Technique must already be solid from Phases 1 and 2 before you push intensity here.

← Back to program overview

Intensity note: Most main lifts move to 5×3 or similar. Prioritize perfect form over adding weight. If form breaks, reduce the load immediately.
Primary compounds

Back Squat · Front Squat · RDL · Pendlay / Barbell Row · Push Press

These are the same patterns from Phase 2, now performed with heavier loads and lower reps (typically 5×3 or 4×4).

Loading principles for Peak phase

  • Choose a weight that leaves 1–2 clean reps in reserve on the first sets.
  • Rest 2.5–3.5 minutes between heavy sets.
  • If the bar speed slows dramatically or form degrades, stop the set.
  • Week 12 is a taper — drop volume and keep intensity moderate on the remaining sets.

Core · Advanced

Hanging Leg Raise

Primary: Lower abs, hip flexors · Secondary: Lats (grip & stability), upper abs

Setup & Execution

  1. Hang from a pull-up bar with an overhand grip, arms fully extended, legs straight or slightly bent.
  2. Brace the core and raise the legs until they are at least parallel to the floor (or higher if strength allows).
  3. Lower under control without swinging. Avoid using momentum.
Key cues
  • “Initiate from the abs, not the hips swinging”
  • “Control the eccentric fully”
  • “Keep the shoulders packed down”
Common mistakes
  • Excessive swinging / kipping
  • Only partial range of motion
  • Letting the shoulders shrug toward the ears
Core · Advanced

Toes-to-Bar

Primary: Abs, hip flexors · Secondary: Lats, grip, upper back

Setup & Execution

  1. Hang from the bar. Create a slight hollow-body position (ribs down, legs slightly in front).
  2. Use a controlled kip or strict movement to bring the toes all the way to the bar.
  3. Touch the bar with the toes, then lower under control back to the hang.
Key cues
  • “Toes to the bar — full contact”
  • “Keep the swing tight and efficient”
  • “Active shoulders throughout”
Common mistakes
  • Wild, uncontrolled swinging
  • Only reaching mid-shin or knees
  • Losing the hollow position and arching hard
Regression: If full toes-to-bar is not yet available, use Hanging Knee Raises or strict Hanging Leg Raises until strength improves.
Core · Anti-extension

Ab Wheel Rollout

Primary: Abs (anti-extension) · Secondary: Lats, shoulders, hip flexors

Setup & Execution

  1. Kneel on a pad holding the ab wheel. Hands under the shoulders, core braced hard.
  2. Slowly roll the wheel forward while keeping the hips and spine from sagging.
  3. Go only as far as you can maintain a rigid torso, then pull the wheel back by contracting the abs and lats.
Key cues
  • “Ribs down, glutes lightly squeezed”
  • “Only as far as you can control”
  • “Pull back with the abs, not just the arms”
Common mistakes
  • Letting the lower back sag (this is the main risk)
  • Going too far too soon
  • Using only the arms to return
Regression: Start with smaller range of motion or use a stability ball rollout until you can control a full ab-wheel range without lumbar extension.
Accessory work still present

Bulgarian Split Squat · Hip Thrust · Pull-ups

These continue from Phase 2. In Peak phase the loads increase and the rep ranges drop (often 3–6 reps). Maintain the same technical standards:

  • Bulgarian Split Squat — most of the load on the front leg, controlled depth, upright torso.
  • Hip Thrust — full glute lockout at the top, ribs down, no lumbar hyperextension.
  • Pull-ups — full hang to chin over the bar, controlled eccentric. Add weight only when you can do clean bodyweight sets.
